\chapter{Experimental Validation}
\label{cap:experiments}

This chapter evaluates the five centerline extractors of Chapter~\ref{cap:geometric_reference}: our two methods, Strip-IRLS and EdgeNet, and the three baselines. Section~\ref{sec:offline_evaluation} compares them offline on masks with a known centerline, and Section~\ref{sec:field_validation} reports the field tests, in which the complete pipeline was tested on the robot, from the camera to the steering commands.

\section{Offline evaluation}
\label{sec:offline_evaluation}

\subsection{Data sets}
\label{subsec:geometric_accuracy}

The offline tests show how each extractor behaves and where it fails. They also compare the extractors, but they do not give a strict ranking. The extractors were tuned only on the synthetic tuning set, 2400 masks (seed 0). With the parameters of Chapter~\ref{cap:geometric_reference} they are tested on the two sets of Table~\ref{tab:c7_datasets}: the synthetic test set, 2400 masks (seed 2027), and all the annotated frames.

The annotated set has 255 frames from 14 sequences of tomato, cucumber and lettuce, all with a corridor. Most sequences come from paths used to train the segmentation network, although the annotated frames themselves were not used in training, so the results can be slightly better than on new paths. On it Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges and Center points have no failures and Column histogram has 6, but these frames are too few to measure the difference. \subsection{Results on the synthetic masks}
\label{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}

The results use three measures:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Error:} $|\hat x-x_{\mathrm{true}}|$ at the row used by the controller, $y_{\mathrm{ref}}=156$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:control_node}), computed only on the masks with a corridor that are not classified as \texttt{lost}.
    \item \textbf{Gross error:} an error above 15~px, 13\% of the half-width of the image.
    \item \textbf{Failure:} a frame that has a corridor but is classified as \texttt{lost}, or a frame with a gross error.
\end{itemize}
The masks of the synthetic test set form three groups:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Base scenarios:} the 19 base scenarios with a corridor, 1900 masks.
    \item \textbf{Adjacent corridors:} the 3 scenarios with adjacent corridors, 300 masks. Navigating in rows with adjacent corridors was not a goal of this work, so they are kept separate.
    \item \textbf{No corridor:} \texttt{empty} and \texttt{full}, 200 masks, where the correct status is \texttt{lost}.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:geometric_extraction_comparison} shows two examples: a synthetic mask where only Center points fails, and a real frame where Column histogram has a gross error.

On the base scenarios Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet and RANSAC edges fail on 13, 2 and 1 of the 1900 masks, Center points on 30 and Column histogram on 458. With adjacent corridors Center points fails on 4 of the 300 masks and Strip-IRLS on 13, EdgeNet returns \texttt{lost} on 25, and RANSAC edges and Column histogram fail on 74 and 72. The causes differ (Table~\ref{tab:c7_scenario_errors}). Strip-IRLS fails mostly when a widening or spots next to the corridor tilt the fit of one edge, and with adjacent corridors when its merging gap joins them to the central one. The 25 \texttt{lost} masks of EdgeNet have a correct line, and they are \texttt{lost} only because of how its confidence is computed. Its training generator is similar to the one of the test set, so the real frames, annotated and in the field, are the fairer test for it. RANSAC edges takes the edge points of an adjacent corridor when it belongs to the same component. Center points starts its single chain from the interval with the most path pixels at the bottom, so a hole or a widening there stops the chain early or moves its first midpoints (Figure~\ref{fig:geometric_extraction_comparison}a). Column histogram returns a vertical line at the column with the most path pixels, so it fails when this column is not at the center of the corridor, for example when the robot is offset or rotated. It has no failures with a widening or spots next to the corridor, where Strip-IRLS and Center points fail. On the mask that is all path (\texttt{full}) Center points and Column histogram return a line with status \texttt{ok}.

All gross errors of RANSAC edges and most of those of Column histogram have status \texttt{ok}, so their status does not flag them. Errors above 30~px come mainly from RANSAC edges and Strip-IRLS with adjacent corridors and from Column histogram in the base scenarios. With $C = 1$ the command saturates at about 54~px (Equation~\eqref{eq:error_saturation}), so a line 30~px off that persists over consecutive frames already changes the command by more than half of its maximum value.

Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges and Center points differ by at most five failures (9 to 14 frames), while Column histogram has 52. EdgeNet has fewer \texttt{lost} frames than the other three (2 against 5 to 8) but more gross errors (12 against 4 to 6). The error is measured only at $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$, as in the controller, so the slope of the line is not evaluated.

\section{Field validation}
\label{sec:field_validation}

The five extractors were tested in closed loop on the robot on 4 November 2026. The robot ran the complete chain of Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}, from the camera to the steering commands, with the segmentation network deployed on the robot and with the debug video enabled. Between runs only the extractor changes. The segmentation network, the controller and the row are the same. The trajectory of the robot was measured with a total station that follows a prism placed at the center of the robot. The centerline of the row is the polyline through five points measured with the same station, with the prism placed by eye on the center of the row. Since the points were placed by hand and the row is not perfectly straight, the centerline is not accurate to the centimeter. The measured lateral distance therefore contains the error of the reference and that of the robot.

\subsection{Test conditions}
\label{subsec:test_conditions}

The tests were made in a row of tomato plants in a greenhouse. It was a difficult case for the segmentation: the plants were sparse, so the predicted path often widened into the gaps between them, and the lighting was poor. No image of this row is in the training data of the segmentation network. The short runs were made at 0.20~m/s and the full row at 0.50~m/s. Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_photo} shows the robot in this row, and Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_frames} a frame of its camera.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{subsec:field_protocol}

Each extractor made two kinds of runs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Short runs:} three runs toward the station, from about 14 to 3~m from it. The robot was placed at the start by hand. The runs are compared between 12 and 4~m from the station. The first part is left out, so that all runs are already under way. The last part is left out because there the robot reaches the end of the row, which is not the aim of these tests.
    \item \textbf{Full row:} one run along the full row, away from the station. The runs are compared from 15 to 39~m from the station.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Results}
\label{subsec:navigation_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_short} shows the lateral distance $d$ from the row centerline along the short runs and Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_full} along the full row. Table~\ref{tab:c7_field} summarizes it with four measures, computed between 4 and 12~m from the station on the short runs and between 15 and 39~m on the full row.

The RMSE shows how close each extractor keeps the robot to the row centerline, the standard deviation between runs how repeatable the extractor is, and the RMSD how similar its trajectory is to those of the other four extractors. The last two measure differences between runs at the same positions along the row, so the error of the row centerline cancels in them, while the RMSE contains it.

For these two measures each run is interpolated linearly at $N$ equally spaced positions: $N=400$ between 4.04 and 11.95~m on the short runs and $N=600$ between 15 and 39~m on the full row. Here $d_{r,i}$ is the distance of run $r=1,\dots,R$ of an extractor at position $i$, with $R=3$ on the short runs and $R=1$ on the full row. At each position, $\mu_i$ is the mean of the $R$ runs of the extractor and $o_i$ is the mean of all the runs of the other four extractors.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RMSE and Max.} For a run with distances $d_1,\dots,d_n$ at the $n$ points measured by the station,
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_j^2},
        \label{eq:c7_rmse}
    \end{equation}
    and the Max is the largest $|d_j|$. Both contain the error of the centerline.
    \item \textbf{Standard deviation between runs.}
    \begin{equation}
        \sigma_{\mathrm{runs}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{RN}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(d_{r,i}-\mu_i\bigr)^2}.
        \label{eq:c7_runs}
    \end{equation}
    It needs more than one run, so it is computed only on the short runs.
    \item \textbf{RMSD.} It compares the mean of the runs of the extractor with that of the other extractors:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{RMSD}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\mu_i-o_i\bigr)^2}.
        \label{eq:c7_rmsd}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
Neither measure shows how close the robot was to the true center of the row, because the part of the lateral distance common to all runs cancels as well.

Column histogram is the exception. Its RMSE is close to that of the others, but its RMSD is 5.1~cm on the short runs and 4.1~cm on the full row, and its standard deviation between runs 4.3~cm. Along the full row it follows the same trajectory as the other extractors, but in some stretches it is up to 10~cm from the mean of the other four, against at most 5~cm for each of them from the mean of the other three. On the short runs each of the other extractors repeats the same curve in its three runs, while the three runs of Column histogram follow three different curves (Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_short}). Where the predicted path covers most of the width of the mask, the column with the most path pixels can lie at the image border, and Column histogram still classifies the frame as \texttt{ok}. Between 4 and 5~m from the station 14\% of its frames have an error $|e_k|$ above 30~px, against at most 3\% for the other extractors.

No extractor is best on all the tests. On the synthetic masks with one corridor, our two methods, Strip-IRLS and EdgeNet, and RANSAC edges have few gross errors (0.1 to 0.3\%), and Center points slightly more (1.0\%). On the annotated frames these four extractors differ by a few frames, which are not independent (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_annotated}). These tests therefore do not rank them. On the Jetson, Strip-IRLS is 1.4 times and EdgeNet 3.3 times faster than RANSAC edges (Table~\ref{tab:c7_summary}). Strip-IRLS has fewer gross errors than RANSAC edges only with adjacent corridors, where Center points has the fewest failures. Table~\ref{tab:c7_summary} gives the gross errors and the \texttt{lost} frames separately, because a \texttt{lost} frame stops the robot after 0.5~s, while a gross error that persists over consecutive frames reaches the command (Section~\ref{subsubsec:control_node}).

In the field the four extractors other than Column histogram drive the robot along almost the same trajectory, so the offline differences between them do not appear in that row. Column histogram has many gross errors in every offline group and the least repeatable runs in the field.

EdgeNet, trained only on synthetic masks, drove the robot in the field along the same trajectory as Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and Center points. On the annotated frames it has the fewest \texttt{lost} frames but more gross errors than Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and Center points (12 against 4 to 6). Its accuracy depends on how well its generator covers the masks met in the field.

Along the full row the lateral distance common to the five extractors stays below 20~cm and repeats at the same positions in all five runs (Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_full}b), so it is not the error of one extractor. It most likely comes from the row, where the plants reach further into the path on one side or the predicted path widens, and from small errors of the reference centerline.

The robot followed the row with each of the five extractors, using the segmentation network trained only on SAM~3 labels without manual correction (Section~\ref{subsec:c4_deployed_network}). This row is not in the training data of the network. On the annotated frames Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet and RANSAC edges fail mostly on the same badly segmented frames (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_annotated}). The quality of the segmentation therefore affects the result more than the choice of the extractor.
