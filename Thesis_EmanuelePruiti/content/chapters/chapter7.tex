\chapter{Experimental Validation}
\label{cap:experiments}

This chapter evaluates the five path extractors of Chapter~\ref{cap:geometric_reference}. Section~\ref{sec:offline_evaluation} compares them offline on masks with a known centerline, and Section~\ref{sec:field_validation} reports the field tests, in which the robot followed a crop row in closed loop. The segmentation networks are evaluated in Chapter~\ref{cap:segmentation}, and the times on the Jetson AGX Orin are in Section~\ref{subsec:timing}.

\section{Offline evaluation}
\label{sec:offline_evaluation}

\subsection{Data sets}
\label{subsec:geometric_accuracy}

The offline tests show how each extractor behaves and where it fails. They also compare the extractors, but they do not give a strict ranking. The extractors were tuned only on the synthetic tuning set, 2400 masks generated with seed 0, by looking at how their errors change with the parameters (Section~\ref{sec:c5_parameter_setting}). With the parameters of Chapter~\ref{cap:geometric_reference} they are tested on the two sets of Table~\ref{tab:c7_datasets}: the synthetic test set, 2400 masks generated with seed 2027, and all the annotated frames. The main test is the field test of Section~\ref{sec:field_validation}, where the robot follows a crop row in closed loop.

The annotated set has 255 frames from 14 sequences of tomato, cucumber and lettuce, all with a corridor. Only one tomato sequence, of 15 frames, comes from a path not used in training. On it Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet and RANSAC edges have no failures, Center points has 2 and Column histogram 6, but these frames are too few to measure the difference. \subsection{Results on the synthetic masks}
\label{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}

The results use three measures:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Error:} $|\hat x-x_{\mathrm{true}}|$ at the row used by the controller, $y_{\mathrm{ref}}=156$ (Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}), computed only on the frames not classified as \texttt{lost}.
    \item \textbf{Gross error:} an error above 15~px, 13\% of the half-width of the image.
    \item \textbf{Failure:} a frame that has a corridor but is classified as \texttt{lost}, or a frame with a gross error.
\end{itemize}
The masks of the synthetic test set form three groups:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Base scenarios:} the 19 base scenarios with a corridor, 1900 masks.
    \item \textbf{Adjacent corridors:} the 3 scenarios with adjacent corridors, 300 masks. Navigating in rows with adjacent corridors was not a goal of this work, so they are kept separate.
    \item \textbf{No corridor:} \texttt{empty} and \texttt{full}, 200 masks, where the correct status is \texttt{lost}.
\end{itemize}
Table~\ref{tab:geometric_benchmark} gives the results of each group, and Table~\ref{tab:c7_scenario_errors} the failures in the scenarios where at least one extractor fails. Figure~\ref{fig:geometric_extraction_comparison} shows two examples: a synthetic mask where only Center points fails, and a real frame where Center points and Column histogram have gross errors.

Each extractor fails in different scenarios:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strip-IRLS.} In \texttt{hole\_bottom\_tall} a central hole 36 to 50~px wide splits the bottom of the corridor. When the hole is wider than the merging gap (30~px) and taller than the seed window (six strips of 10 rows), the chain starts in one of the two parts and follows its edges until the parts join, where the interval widens beyond the jump limit. In \texttt{adjacent+offset} the merging gap joins the narrow upper part of the crop row between two corridors with the corridors beside it.
    \item \textbf{EdgeNet.} It fails only in \texttt{bulge\_\allowbreak one\_\allowbreak side} (2 gross errors) and in \texttt{adjacent\_\allowbreak narrow\_\allowbreak crop}, where it returns \texttt{lost} for 25 masks. In these 25 masks its lines are correct (error at most 2.5~px), but the adjacent corridors are path pixels outside them, which lower the IoU, and the confidence falls below 0.38. For the same reason it classifies only 26 of the 300 masks with adjacent corridors as \texttt{ok}, although it has no gross errors on them. These results can be favorable to EdgeNet. Its generator draws adjacent corridors with the same vanishing point and rows 5 to 150~px wide, so these scenarios are not new to it, and its loss gives double weight to the centerline at row 156, the row where the error is measured.
    \item \textbf{RANSAC edges.} It takes the first and last path pixel of each row of the selected component. When an adjacent corridor belongs to that component, its edge replaces the edge of the central corridor in those rows.
    \item \textbf{Center points.} It forms the midpoints of the intervals before the fit, so a disturbance on one side of the corridor moves the midpoints and the centerline (\texttt{bulge\_one\_side}, Figure~\ref{fig:geometric_extraction_comparison}a). With adjacent corridors it follows one interval per strip and has almost no gross errors.
    \item \textbf{Column histogram.} It returns a vertical line, so its error grows with the offset and the rotation of the robot.
\end{itemize}
On the mask that is all path (\texttt{full}) Center points and Column histogram return a line with status \texttt{ok}. A check on the share of path pixels in the ROI could reject this mask.

The gross errors also differ in size (Table~\ref{tab:geometric_benchmark}). Errors above 30~px come mainly from RANSAC edges and Strip-IRLS with adjacent corridors, where they follow an adjacent corridor, and from Column histogram in the base scenarios. With $K_p = 1.2$ and $C = 1$ the command of the controller saturates at about 54~px (Equation~\eqref{eq:error_saturation}), so a line 30~px off already changes the command by more than half of its maximum value.

Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet have the fewest failures and differ by at most five frames. EdgeNet has fewer \texttt{lost} frames than the other two (2 against 5) but more gross errors (12 against 6 and 4). All gross errors of Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges and 8 of the 12 of EdgeNet are classified as \texttt{ok}, so their status does not warn the controller. Only one gross error is above 50~px (Center points, 57~px). Of the 22 gross errors of Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet, 19 are on the 133 tomato frames. They are on only 15 different frames, so the three extractors fail mostly on the same masks. On these frames the segmentation is much worse than on the others, as visual inspection of the masks shows.

These results have four limits. The frames of a sequence are not independent, so the 255 frames count as fewer independent cases. The edges were marked by hand, which does not give a precision of a few pixels. The median error therefore does not show which extractor is more precise, and the comparison relies on the gross errors. The error is measured only at $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$, as in the controller, so the slope of the line is not evaluated. Finally, the frames where the edges could not be marked or gave no usable centerline are not in the set.

\section{Field validation}
\label{sec:field_validation}

The five extractors were tested in closed loop on the robot on 4 November 2026. The robot ran the complete chain of Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}, from the camera to the steering commands, with the segmentation network deployed on the robot and with the debug video enabled. Between runs only the extractor changes. The segmentation network, the controller and the row are the same. Its trajectory was measured with a total station that follows a prism placed at the center of the robot. The centerline of the row is the polyline through five points measured with the same station, with the prism placed by eye on the center of the row. Since the points were placed by hand and the row is not perfectly straight, the centerline and the row edges are not accurate to the centimeter. The measured lateral distance therefore contains the error of the reference and that of the robot.

\subsection{Test conditions}
\label{subsec:test_conditions}

The tests were made in a row of tomato plants in a tunnel greenhouse. It was a difficult case for the segmentation: the plants were sparse, so the predicted path often widened into the gaps between them, and the lighting was poor. No image of this field is in the training data of the segmentation network. Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_photo} shows the robot in this row, and Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_frames} a frame of its camera.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{subsec:field_protocol}

Each extractor made two kinds of runs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Short runs:} three runs toward the station, from about 14 to 3~m from it. The robot was placed at the start by hand. The runs are compared between 12 and 4~m from the station. The first part is left out, so that all runs are already under way. The last part is left out because there the robot reaches the end of the row, which is not the aim of these tests.
    \item \textbf{Full row:} one run along the full row, away from the station. The runs are compared from 15 to 39~m from the station.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Results}
\label{subsec:navigation_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_short} shows the lateral distance $d$ from the row centerline along the short runs and Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_full} along the full row. Table~\ref{tab:c7_field} summarizes it with four measures, computed between 4 and 12~m from the station on the short runs and between 15 and 39~m on the full row.

The standard deviation between runs and the RMSD compare runs at the same positions along the row. Each run is interpolated linearly at $N$ equally spaced positions: $N=400$ between 4.04 and 11.95~m on the short runs and $N=600$ between 15 and 39~m on the full row. Here $d_{r,i}$ is the distance of run $r=1,\dots,R$ of an extractor at position $i$, with $R=3$ on the short runs and $R=1$ on the full row. At each position, $\mu_i$ is the mean of the $R$ runs of the extractor and $o_i$ is the mean of all the runs of the other four extractors. At a given position the error of the centerline is the same in all runs, so it cancels in both measures.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RMSE and Max: distance from the centerline.} For a run with distances $d_1,\dots,d_n$ at the $n$ points measured by the station,
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_j^2},
        \label{eq:c7_rmse}
    \end{equation}
    and the Max is the largest $|d_j|$. Both contain the error of the centerline.
    \item \textbf{Standard deviation between runs: how repeatable an extractor is.} It says how much the runs of the extractor differ from each other:
    \begin{equation}
        \sigma_{\mathrm{runs}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{RN}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(d_{r,i}-\mu_i\bigr)^2}.
        \label{eq:c7_runs}
    \end{equation}
    It needs more than one run, so it is computed only on the short runs.
    \item \textbf{RMSD: how much an extractor differs from the others.} It compares the mean of the runs of the extractor with that of the other extractors:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{RMSD}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\mu_i-o_i\bigr)^2}.
        \label{eq:c7_rmsd}
    \end{equation}
    A small RMSD means that, on average, the extractor drives the robot along the same path as the other extractors.
\end{itemize}
Averaging the runs first keeps $\sigma_{\mathrm{runs}}^2$ out of the RMSD. Comparing each run directly with $o_i$ would give both:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{RN}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(d_{r,i}-o_i\bigr)^2=\mathrm{RMSD}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{runs}}^2.
    \label{eq:c7_split}
\end{equation}
Neither measure shows how close the robot was to the true center of the row, because the part of the lateral distance common to all runs cancels as well.

Column histogram is the exception. Its RMSE is close to that of the others, but its three short runs follow different curves, with a standard deviation between runs of 4.3~cm and an RMSD of 5.1~cm on the short runs and 4.1~cm on the full row. Where the predicted path covers most of the width of the mask, the column with the most path pixels can lie at the image border, and Column histogram still classifies the frame as \texttt{ok}. Its large errors are near the end of the short runs. Between 4 and 7~m from the station, 3 to 14\% of its frames in each meter have a measured lateral error $|e_k|$ above 30~px, against at most 3\% for the other extractors. Between about 7 and 5.5~m the predicted path is wider in all runs, so there the segmentation is harder for all the extractors.

No extractor is best on all the tests. On the tests within the aim of this work, the synthetic masks with one corridor and the annotated frames, Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet and RANSAC edges have few gross errors, and on the annotated frames they differ by a few frames, which are not independent (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_annotated}). These tests therefore do not rank these three extractors. Strip-IRLS has fewer gross errors than RANSAC edges only with adjacent corridors. Center points has more gross errors and, on the annotated frames, many more \texttt{lost} frames. Table~\ref{tab:c7_summary} gives the two separately, because a \texttt{lost} frame stops the robot after 0.5~s, while a gross error that persists over consecutive frames reaches the command (Section~\ref{subsubsec:control_node}).

In the field the four extractors other than Column histogram drive the robot along almost the same path, so the offline differences between them do not appear in that row. Column histogram has the most gross errors offline and the least repeatable runs in the field.

EdgeNet, trained only on synthetic masks, drove the robot in the field along the same path as the geometric extractors. On the annotated frames it has the fewest \texttt{lost} frames but more gross errors than Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges (12 against 6 and 4). Its accuracy depends on how well its generator covers the masks met in the field.

The lateral distance common to the four similar extractors stays below 20~cm and repeats at the same positions in all their runs, so it is not the error of one extractor. It comes from the row, where the plants reach further into the path on one side or the predicted path widens, and from small errors of the reference centerline.

The segmentation network deployed on the robot, trained only on SAM~3 labels without manual correction (Section~\ref{subsec:c4_deployed_network}), guided the robot along the row with all five extractors, in a field not present in its training data. The best extractors fail mostly on the same badly segmented frames (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_annotated}), so the quality of the segmentation matters more than the choice of the extractor.
