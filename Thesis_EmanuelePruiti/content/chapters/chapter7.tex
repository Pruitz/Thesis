\chapter{Experimental Validation}
\label{cap:experiments}

This chapter evaluates the five path extractors of Chapter~\ref{cap:geometric_reference}. Section~\ref{sec:offline_evaluation} compares them offline on masks with a known centerline, and Section~\ref{sec:field_validation} reports the field tests, in which the robot followed a crop row in closed loop. The segmentation networks are evaluated in Chapter~\ref{cap:segmentation}, and the times on the Jetson AGX Orin are in Section~\ref{subsec:timing}.

\section{Offline evaluation}
\label{sec:offline_evaluation}

\subsection{Data sets}
\label{subsec:geometric_accuracy}

The offline tests show how each extractor behaves and where it fails. They also compare the extractors, but they do not give a strict ranking. The extractors were tuned only on the synthetic tuning set, 2400 masks generated with seed 0, by looking at how their errors change with the parameters (Section~\ref{sec:c5_parameter_setting}). With the parameters of Chapter~\ref{cap:geometric_reference} they are tested on the two sets of Table~\ref{tab:c7_datasets}: the synthetic test set, 2400 masks generated with seed 2027, and all the annotated frames. The main test is the field test of Section~\ref{sec:field_validation}, where the robot follows a crop row in closed loop.

The synthetic masks have $224\times224$ pixels. Each one is a corridor in perspective, defined by its bottom center, vanishing point, bottom half-width and apex row, with irregular edges (a slow undulation of a few pixels and a fine grain of 1--2~px). The annotated set has 255 frames from 14 sequences of tomato, cucumber and lettuce, all with a corridor. Most sequences come from paths used to train the segmentation network, although the annotated frames themselves were not used in training, so the results can be slightly better than on new paths. Only one tomato sequence, of 15 frames, comes from a path not used in training. On it Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet and RANSAC edges have no failures, Center points has 2 and Column histogram 6, but these frames are too few to measure the difference. The masks were predicted by the network deployed on the robot, and the extractors run on them as on the robot. The edges were marked by hand on these masks, so the error does not contain the segmentation error.

\subsection{Results on the synthetic masks}
\label{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}

The results use three measures:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Error:} $|\hat x-x_{\mathrm{true}}|$ at the row used by the controller, $y_{\mathrm{ref}}=156$ (Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}), computed only on the frames not classified as \texttt{lost}.
    \item \textbf{Gross error:} an error above 15~px, 13\% of the half-width of the image.
    \item \textbf{Failure:} a frame that has a corridor but is classified as \texttt{lost}, or a frame with a gross error.
\end{itemize}
The masks of the synthetic test set form three groups:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Base scenarios:} the 19 base scenarios with a corridor, 1900 masks.
    \item \textbf{Adjacent corridors:} the 3 scenarios with adjacent corridors, 300 masks. Navigating in rows with adjacent corridors was not a goal of this work, so they are kept separate.
    \item \textbf{No corridor:} \texttt{empty} and \texttt{full}, 200 masks, where the correct status is \texttt{lost}.
\end{itemize}
Table~\ref{tab:geometric_benchmark} gives the results of each group, and Table~\ref{tab:c7_scenario_errors} the failures in the scenarios where at least one extractor fails. Figure~\ref{fig:geometric_extraction_comparison} shows two examples: a synthetic mask where only Center points fails, and a real frame where Center points and Column histogram have gross errors.

Each extractor fails in different scenarios:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strip-IRLS.} In \texttt{hole\_bottom\_tall} a central hole 36 to 50~px wide splits the bottom of the corridor. When the hole is wider than the merging gap (30~px) and taller than the seed window (six strips of 10 rows), the chain starts in one of the two parts and follows its edges until the parts join, where the interval widens beyond the jump limit. In \texttt{adjacent+offset} the merging gap joins the narrow upper part of the crop row between two corridors with the corridors beside it.
    \item \textbf{EdgeNet.} It fails only in \texttt{bulge\_\allowbreak one\_\allowbreak side} (2 gross errors) and in \texttt{adjacent\_\allowbreak narrow\_\allowbreak crop}, where it returns \texttt{lost} for 25 masks. In these 25 masks its lines are correct (error at most 2.5~px), but the adjacent corridors are path pixels outside them, which lower the IoU, and the confidence falls below 0.38. For the same reason it classifies only 26 of the 300 masks with adjacent corridors as \texttt{ok}, although it has no gross errors on them.
    \item \textbf{RANSAC edges.} It takes the first and last path pixel of each row of the selected component. When an adjacent corridor belongs to that component, its edge replaces the edge of the central corridor in those rows.
    \item \textbf{Center points.} It forms the midpoints of the intervals before the fit, so a disturbance on one side of the corridor moves the midpoints and the centerline (\texttt{bulge\_one\_side}, Figure~\ref{fig:geometric_extraction_comparison}a). With adjacent corridors it follows one interval per strip and has almost no gross errors.
    \item \textbf{Column histogram.} It returns a vertical line, so its error grows with the offset and the rotation of the robot.
\end{itemize}
On the mask that is all path (\texttt{full}) Center points and Column histogram return a line with status \texttt{ok}. A check on the share of path pixels in the ROI could reject this mask.

The gross errors also differ in size (Table~\ref{tab:geometric_benchmark}). Errors above 30~px come mainly from RANSAC edges and Strip-IRLS with adjacent corridors, where they follow an adjacent corridor, and from Column histogram in the base scenarios. With $K_p = 1.2$ and $C = 1$ the command of the controller saturates at about 54~px (Equation~\eqref{eq:error_saturation}), so a line 30~px off already changes the command by more than half of its maximum value.

Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet have the fewest failures and differ by at most five frames. EdgeNet has fewer \texttt{lost} frames than the other two (2 against 5) but more gross errors (12 against 6 and 4). All gross errors of Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges and 8 of the 12 of EdgeNet are classified as \texttt{ok}, so their status does not warn the controller. Only one gross error is above 50~px (Center points, 57~px). Of the 22 gross errors of Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet, 19 are on the 133 tomato frames. They are on only 15 different frames, so the three extractors fail mostly on the same masks. On these frames the segmentation is much worse than on the others, as visual inspection of the masks shows.

These results have four limits. The frames of a sequence are not independent, so the 255 frames count as fewer independent cases. The edges were marked by hand, which does not give a precision of a few pixels. The median error therefore does not show which extractor is more precise, and the comparison relies on the gross errors. The error is measured only at $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$, as in the controller, so the slope of the line is not evaluated. Finally, the frames where the edges could not be marked or gave no usable centerline are not in the set.

\section{Field validation}
\label{sec:field_validation}

The five extractors were tested in closed loop on the robot on 4 November 2026. The robot ran the complete chain of Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}, from the camera to the steering commands, with the segmentation network deployed on the robot and with the debug video enabled. Its trajectory was measured with a total station that follows a prism placed at the center of the robot, in a horizontal frame where $Y$ runs approximately along the row and $X$ across it. The centerline of the row is the polyline through five points measured with the same station, with the prism placed by eye on the center of the row. The lateral distance of the robot is its perpendicular distance from this polyline, positive when the robot is on the $+X$ side of it, in both directions of travel. This reference has its own error. The points were placed by eye, and the row is not straight: the intermediate points lie up to 6.3~cm from the line through the first and the last point. The measured lateral distance therefore contains the error of the reference and that of the robot.

\subsection{Test conditions}
\label{subsec:test_conditions}

The tests were made in a row of tomato plants in a tunnel greenhouse. It was a difficult case for the segmentation: the plants were sparse, so the predicted path often widened into the gaps between them, and the lighting was poor. No image of this field is in the training data of the segmentation network. Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_photo} shows the robot in this row, and Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_frames}a a frame of its camera.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{images/field/field_robot_row.jpg}
  \caption{The robot in the tomato row of the field tests.}
  \label{fig:c7_field_photo}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{subsec:field_protocol}

Each extractor made two kinds of runs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Short runs:} three runs toward the station, from about $Y=14$~m to $Y=3$~m. The robot was placed at the start by hand. The runs are compared from $Y=12.0$ to $Y=4.0$~m. The first part is left out, so that all runs are already under way. The last part is left out because there the robot reaches the end of the row, which is not the aim of these tests.
    \item \textbf{Full row:} one run along the full row, away from the station. The runs are compared from $Y=14$ to about $Y=40$~m.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Results}
\label{subsec:navigation_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_short} shows the lateral distance along the short runs and Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_full} along the full row. Table~\ref{tab:c7_field} summarizes it for all runs. It gives the root mean square of the lateral distance (RMSE), as in the field tests of Navone et al.~\cite[Table~4]{navone2025gpsfree}. All runs are measured from the same reference, placed by eye, so the table also gives two measures that compare the runs with each other: the difference from the other extractors and, for the short runs, the repeatability.

Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges and Center points give almost the same trajectory. Their RMSE is 5.3 to 5.7~cm on the short runs and 8.6 to 9.4~cm on the full row, but each of them differs from the mean of the other three by only 0.7 to 1.8~cm. The lateral distance they have in common varies from $-1.5$ to $+10.4$~cm on the short runs and from $-6.5$ to $+17.9$~cm on the full row. Their RMSE therefore comes mostly from this common part, and these runs do not rank them. In both directions of travel the common part is mostly toward $+X$, so it comes mainly from the field, from the plants seen by the camera or from the reference. Each of their short runs differs from the mean of the three runs of the same extractor by 0.3 to 0.6~cm, so each of them repeats its trajectory.

Column histogram has an RMSE close to the others, but it differs from them by 6.8~cm on the short runs and 4.1~cm on the full row, and its repeatability is 4.4~cm. It classified every frame between $Y=12.0$ and $Y=5.0$~m as \texttt{ok}, also when its centerline was at the image border (Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_frames}b and~c). Where the predicted path covers most of the width of the mask, the column with the most path pixels can lie at the border, and the measured error reaches about $\pm110$~px. This gives its largest distance, 13.6~cm, at the end of Run~2.

No extractor is best on all the tests. On the annotated frames Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet have the fewest failures, and on the synthetic masks each extractor has its own failure case (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}). EdgeNet has few gross errors, but it classifies 274 of the 300 masks with adjacent corridors as \texttt{degraded} or \texttt{lost}. In the field, Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges and Center points differ from each other by 0.7 to 1.8~cm (root mean square), so the offline differences between them do not appear in that row. Column histogram has the most failures on the base synthetic scenarios and is the only extractor that differs in the field.

The controller ignores a jump of the error on a single frame (Eq.~\eqref{eq:filter_jump}), but a wrong line that persists over consecutive frames reaches the command. A \texttt{lost} frame does not reach the command: the controller keeps the last filtered error for up to 0.5~s and then stops the robot (Section~\ref{subsubsec:control_node}). For Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet such a line is usually classified as \texttt{ok}, so the status does not warn the controller. A confidence calibrated on real frames could do it.

On the annotated frames it is close to the geometric extractors but not better. Its accuracy depends on how well its generator covers the masks met in the field. Adapting it to a new crop or camera mounting means changing the generator and retraining the network, not tuning thresholds in pixels. The design choices are moved into the generator, which has 16 disturbance probabilities and many hand-chosen ranges, and the status still depends on the thresholds 0.5, 8~px, 0.38 and 0.60. The geometric extractors also use one set of parameters for the three crops, so this is not an advantage of the network alone. The network was not tested with another camera or mounting. Its results in these tests can be favorable to it, because its loss gives double weight to row 156, the row where the error is measured. Its generator also draws adjacent corridors with the same vanishing point and rows 5 to 150~px wide, so the adjacent scenarios of the test are not new to it.

In the field tests the extractor changes between runs, while the segmentation network, the controller and the row are the same. The lateral distance common to the four similar extractors varies by about 24~cm along the full row and repeats at the same positions in all their runs, so it comes from something fixed along the row. It can come from the masks predicted in this field, with sparse plants and poor light, or from the reference, which is placed by eye and does not follow the row between its five points. These measurements cannot separate the two causes. If the common part comes mainly from the masks, as the conditions of this field suggest, a better segmentation of such fields would change the trajectory more than a change among these four extractors. A reference measured at more points along the row would reduce the part of the measured distance that does not come from the robot.
