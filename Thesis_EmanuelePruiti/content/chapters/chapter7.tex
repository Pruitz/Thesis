\chapter{Experimental Validation}
\label{cap:experiments}

This chapter evaluates the five path extractors of Chapter~\ref{cap:geometric_reference}. Section~\ref{sec:offline_evaluation} compares them offline on masks with a known centerline, and Section~\ref{sec:field_validation} reports the field tests, in which the robot followed a crop row in closed loop. The segmentation networks are evaluated in Chapter~\ref{cap:segmentation}, and the times on the Jetson AGX Orin are in Section~\ref{subsec:timing}.

\section{Offline evaluation}
\label{sec:offline_evaluation}

\subsection{Data sets}
\label{subsec:geometric_accuracy}

The extractors were tuned only on the synthetic masks of seed 0 (Section~\ref{sec:c5_parameter_setting}). They are tested on the synthetic masks of seed 2027 and on all the annotated frames.

Each extractor fails in different scenarios:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strip-IRLS.} In \texttt{hole\_bottom\_tall} a central hole 33 to 50~px wide splits the bottom of the corridor. Where the hole is wider than the merging gap (30~px) and taller than the seed window (six strips of 10 rows), every seed lies in one of the two parts. The chain stops where the parts join, because there the interval widens beyond the jump limit, and its edges are the two edges of one part. This gives gross errors in 2.0\% of the masks. In \texttt{adjacent+offset} the merging gap joins the narrow upper part of the row between two corridors with the corridors beside it (4.2\%).
    \item \textbf{EdgeNet.} In the base scenarios it has two gross errors, both in \texttt{bulge\_\allowbreak one\_\allowbreak side}. With adjacent corridors it has no gross errors, but it classifies only 26 of these 300 masks as \texttt{ok}, because the mask also contains the adjacent corridors and the IoU in its confidence is low. A first model, trained with an earlier generator without adjacent corridors and with the apex only between rows 30 and 100, had 95.7\% gross errors in \texttt{adjacent+offset}.
    \item \textbf{RANSAC edges.} It takes the first and last path pixel of each row of the selected component. When an adjacent corridor belongs to that component, its edge replaces the edge of the central corridor in those rows.
    \item \textbf{Center points.} It forms the midpoints of the intervals before the fit, so a disturbance on one side of the corridor moves the midpoints and enters the centerline (\texttt{bulge\_one\_side}). It returns a line on the mask that is all path. With adjacent corridors it follows one interval per strip and has almost no gross errors.
    \item \textbf{Column histogram.} It returns a vertical line, so its error grows with the offset and the rotation of the robot.
\end{itemize}

Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges have the fewest failures, 11 and 9 of 255, and two frames do not separate them. EdgeNet fails on 14 frames, Center points and Column histogram on 52 each. All gross errors of Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges and 8 of the 12 of EdgeNet are classified as \texttt{ok}, so their status does not warn the controller.

These results have five limits. The frames of a sequence are not independent. Of the 22 gross errors of Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet, 19 are on tomato frames, so the comparison between them rests mainly on the 133 tomato frames. The annotation was made once by one person, with the predicted mask shown in overlay, which can draw the annotation toward the mask, and its error was not measured. For this reason the differences of a few pixels in the median error are not interpreted, and the comparison relies on the gross errors. The ground truth is marked on the photo, so the measured error also contains the segmentation error, which is the same for all extractors. Finally, the frames where the edges could not be marked or gave no usable centerline are not in the set. They are among the most difficult, so the reported rates are lower than on all frames.

\section{Field validation}
\label{sec:field_validation}

The five extractors were tested in closed loop on the robot on 4 November 2026. The robot ran the complete chain of Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}, from the camera to the steering commands, with the segmentation network deployed on the robot and with the debug video enabled. Its trajectory was measured with a total station.

\subsection{Test conditions}
\label{subsec:test_conditions}

The tests were made in a row of tomato plants. It was a difficult case for the segmentation: the plants were sparse, so the predicted path often widened into the gaps between them, and the lighting was poor. No image of this field is in the training data of the segmentation network. Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_frames}a shows a frame of this row.

\subsection{Trajectory measurement}
\label{subsec:total_station}

The position of the robot was measured with a total station that follows a prism automatically. The prism was placed by hand at the lateral center of the robot. The station records about four positions per second, as coordinates $(X,Y)$ in a horizontal frame with the origin at the station, in which $Y$ runs approximately along the row.

The reference for the center of the row is a set of five points measured with the same station, with the prism placed by hand on the center of the row, at $Y=1.55$, $18.09$, $27.09$, $32.38$ and $39.88$~m. The centerline is the polyline through these points, with the first and the last segment extended. The lateral distance of the robot is its perpendicular distance from this polyline, positive when the robot is to the left of the centerline with respect to its direction of travel.

This reference has two limits. The points were placed by eye, so their position across the row is not exact, and their error was not measured. The row is not straight: the three intermediate points lie 4.0 to 6.3~cm from the line through the first and the last point, and between two points the polyline does not follow the row. The short runs described below lie entirely between the first two points, 16.5~m apart, where the reference is a single straight segment. The measured lateral distance therefore contains the error of the reference as well as the error of the robot.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{subsec:field_protocol}

Each extractor made three short runs toward the station. The robot was placed at the start by hand, at about $Y=13.9$~m, with a lateral distance from the centerline between $-9.7$ and $+7.3$~cm. In RANSAC edges Run~3 the robot started at $Y=12.97$~m and moved forward and backward before the run, and only the part after this movement is used. At $Y=12.0$~m the twelve runs of Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges and Center points lie within 1.8~cm of each other. The short runs are therefore compared from $Y=12.0$ to $Y=4.0$~m (8.0~m), so that the start position does not enter the comparison.

After the short runs, each extractor made one run along the full row, away from the station. The full-row runs are compared from $Y=14$ to about $Y=39.7$~m, where the station tracked the prism in all five runs (in those of EdgeNet and Column histogram only from $Y=9.96$~m and $Y=13.94$~m).

On the short runs their mean lateral distance is between $+2.9$ and $+4.2$~cm, and the largest absolute value of each extractor is between 10.1 and 11.0~cm. On the full row the mean is between $-6.2$ and $-7.2$~cm and the largest absolute value between 16.3 and 20.4~cm. Their correlation with the mean of the other extractors is $-0.88$ to $0.40$, against 0.94 to 0.98 for the runs of the other four. The data recorded by the controller show the cause. Between $Y=12.0$ and $Y=5.0$~m, Column histogram classified every frame of its three runs as \texttt{ok}, also when its centerline was at the image border. Where the predicted path covers most of the width of the mask, the column with the most path pixels can lie at the border, and the measured lateral error reaches about $\pm110$~px, almost the half-width of the mask (112~px). Figure~\ref{fig:c7_field_frames}b and~c shows two of these frames. The filtered error is still 28 and 32~px in these two frames. In Run~2 the measured error is on average about $+20$~px between $Y=5.25$ and $Y=4.75$~m, and the robot turns to the right. This gives the largest distance of Column histogram in Table~\ref{tab:c7_field}, 13.6~cm, at the end of Run~2.

Between $Y=5.0$ and $Y=4.0$~m Strip-IRLS classified 232 of 241 frames as \texttt{ok}, with a mean absolute measured error of 1.5 to 4.8~px in each half meter of its three runs. The confidence of EdgeNet falls there: between $Y=4.5$ and $Y=4.0$~m, 46 of the 84 frames of its two recorded runs are \texttt{degraded}. In the masks computed offline from the recorded images, the predicted path ends at about row 133, below the station and the operator, while the edge lines of EdgeNet continue upward. The IoU of Eq.~\eqref{eq:c5_edgenet_iou} falls to about 0.6, while $p_{\mathrm{valid}}$ stays at 1 (Figure~\ref{fig:c5_edgenet_examples}f). A lower confidence is expected there, since the station and the operator stand on the path.

No extractor is best on all the tests. On the annotated frames Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges have the fewest failures, and on the synthetic masks each extractor has its own failure case (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}). EdgeNet has few gross errors, but it classifies 274 of the 300 masks with adjacent corridors as \texttt{degraded} or \texttt{lost}. In the field, Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges and Center points differ from each other by 1.1 to 2.1~cm (root mean square), so the offline differences between them do not appear in that row. Column histogram has the most failures on the base synthetic scenarios and is the only extractor that differs in the field.

The controller ignores a jump of the error on a single frame (Eq.~\eqref{eq:filter_jump}), but a wrong line that persists over consecutive frames reaches the command. For Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet such a line is usually classified as \texttt{ok}, so the status does not warn the controller. A confidence calibrated on real frames could do it.

The results of EdgeNet are those of its second trained model. On the annotated frames it is close to the geometric extractors but not better. Its accuracy depends on how well its generator covers the masks met in the field, as the first model, trained without adjacent corridors, showed (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}). Adapting it to a new crop or camera mounting means changing the generator and retraining the network, not tuning thresholds in pixels. The design choices are moved into the generator, which has 16 disturbance probabilities and many hand-chosen ranges, and the status still depends on the thresholds 0.5, 8~px, 0.38 and 0.60. The geometric extractors also use one set of parameters for the three crops, so this is not an advantage of the network alone. The network was not tested with another camera or mounting. Two choices may favor it in these tests. Its loss gives double weight to row 156, the row of the evaluation. The second generator was written after the errors of the first model had been inspected on masks of the real sequences, which include the sequences of the annotated frames but not their annotations.

In the field tests the extractor changes between runs, while the segmentation network, the controller and the row are the same. The lateral distance common to the four similar extractors varies by about 24~cm along the full row and repeats at the same positions in all their runs, so it comes from something fixed along the row. It can come from the masks predicted in this field, with sparse plants and poor light, or from the reference, which is placed by eye and does not follow the row between its five points. These measurements cannot separate the two causes. If the common part comes mainly from the masks, as the conditions of this field suggest, a better segmentation of such fields would change the trajectory more than a change among these four extractors. A reference measured at more points along the row would reduce the part of the measured distance that does not come from the robot.
