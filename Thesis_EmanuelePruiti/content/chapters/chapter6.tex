\chapter{Geometric Extraction of the Local Navigation Reference}
\label{cap:geometric_reference}

This chapter describes the path extractor used on the robot, implemented in \texttt{pf5.py} and called Method~4 in the experimental evaluation, and the three baseline extractors it is compared with. Method~4 extracts the path intervals in horizontal strips of the predicted mask and, starting from the bottom of the image, selects in each strip the interval that continues the corridor found below, keeping the chain of intervals that gives the most edge samples. It then fits the left and right edges of the corridor separately with iteratively reweighted least squares. The centerline is the mean of the two edge lines.

\section{Edge and centerline models}
\label{sec:c6_input}

The input is the label mask $M$ predicted by the network, of height $H$ and width $W$, with $x$ increasing to the right and $y$ downward. In the mask predicted by the binary network, the path pixels have value 1 and all other pixels have value 0. The two corridor edges are modeled as straight lines in the image,
\begin{equation}
    x_L(y)=m_Ly+q_L, \qquad x_R(y)=m_Ry+q_R,
    \label{eq:c6_edges}
\end{equation}
and the centerline is their mean,
\begin{equation}
    x_C(y)=\frac{x_L(y)+x_R(y)}{2}=m_Cy+q_C,
    \qquad m_C=\frac{m_L+m_R}{2},\quad q_C=\frac{q_L+q_R}{2}.
    \label{eq:c6_line}
\end{equation}
Writing $x$ as a function of $y$ avoids infinite slopes for vertical lines. Method~4 estimates two separate lines, each with its own slope and intercept. The edges need not be parallel, so the modeled width can decrease toward the top of the image because of perspective. The two lines can also represent a lateral offset or a heading error of the robot with respect to the row. All distances are in pixels of the input mask.

Each edge is fitted on its own samples with its own weights, so within the fit a sample far from one edge does not change the line of the other edge.

The processing stages are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Select a lower region of interest and clean the path mask (Section~\ref{sec:c6_roi}).
    \item Extract the path intervals in horizontal strips (Section~\ref{sec:c6_candidates}).
    \item Associate intervals upward from each candidate seed and keep the best chain (Section~\ref{sec:c6_selection}).
    \item Fit the two edges separately (Section~\ref{sec:c6_fitting}).
    \item Validate the edge geometry, assign a status from a confidence score, and return the centerline (Section~\ref{sec:c6_output}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Region of interest and mask cleaning}
\label{sec:c6_roi}

The region of interest (ROI) begins at $y_0=\lfloor\rho H\rfloor$ and extends to the bottom of the image, with height $H_R=H-y_0$. The ratio $\rho$ depends on the camera mounting and framing. It is set so that the ROI keeps only the part of the image that contains the path. On the robot, $\rho=0.50$, so for a $224\times224$ mask the ROI covers rows 112 to 223.

The path pixels of $M$ inside the ROI form a binary mask. It is cleaned with an opening ($5\times5$ elliptical kernel) followed by a closing ($9\times9$ rectangular kernel), and connected components with fewer than 500 pixels are removed. All larger components are kept. The resulting mask $\widetilde M$ (\texttt{path} in the code) is used for the interval extraction.

\section{Horizontal strips and intervals}
\label{sec:c6_candidates}

The ROI is divided into $N$ strips of height $h=\lfloor H_R/N\rfloor$, indexed from the bottom. $N$ is a parameter of the extractor, set to 16 on the robot. Strip $s$ covers the ROI rows from $H_R-(s+1)h$ to $H_R-sh-1$, and its height in the image is $y_0+H_R-(s+0.5)h$. On the robot, $h=7$~px, so the strips cover all 112 rows of the ROI.

In each strip, a column is active if it contains at least one path pixel of $\widetilde M$. Runs of active columns separated by at most 30 inactive columns are merged into intervals $[\ell_i,r_i]$ of width $d_i=r_i-\ell_i+1$. Intervals narrower than 8~px are discarded. An endpoint within 2~px of the image border is flagged as clipped. An interval clipped on both sides is discarded when path pixels cover less than 70\% of the strip.

\section{Bottom-up association and chain selection}
\label{sec:c6_selection}

The association starts from a seed interval and moves upward. In each strip, each candidate interval is compared with the last accepted interval $[\ell_p,r_p]$ of width $d_p$ through the boundary displacement $\delta_i=\max(|\ell_i-\ell_p|,|r_i-r_p|)$. Among the candidates with $\delta_i\le D_4$, the one with the lowest cost $J_{4,i}$ is accepted:
\begin{equation}
    D_4=\max(28,\,0.5\,d_p),\qquad J_{4,i}=\delta_i+0.4\,|d_i-d_p|.
    \label{eq:c6_cost4}
\end{equation}
A strip without a compatible candidate is skipped and the last accepted interval is kept as reference. The association stops at the fourth consecutive skipped strip (\texttt{max\_gap\_strips}=3). It is greedy and carries no information from one frame to the next.

Every interval of the bottom six strips is used as a seed, and each seed produces a chain of accepted intervals. A seed that already belongs to a chain found from a lower seed is skipped, because its chain would be the upper part of that chain. The selected chain is the one with the most endpoints not clipped at the image border, which are the samples available to the two edge fits (Section~\ref{sec:c6_fitting}). Ties are broken by the lowest sum of the costs $J_{4,i}$. An interval clipped on both sides adds no samples, and an interval clipped on one side adds one sample, so these chains receive fewer samples than a chain of the same length along the corridor. If the selected chain has fewer than three intervals, the result is \texttt{lost}.

On the robot, the six-strip seed window covers 42 image rows and the three tolerated skipped strips cover 21 rows. Changing $N$ or $\rho$ changes $h$ and therefore both extents.

\section{Separate fitting of the corridor edges}
\label{sec:c6_fitting}

The left edge is fitted on the left endpoints $\ell_i$ of the accepted intervals that are not clipped on the left, and the right edge on the right endpoints $r_i$ that are not clipped on the right. Each edge needs at least two samples, otherwise the result is \texttt{lost}. There is no single-edge fallback. The two fits can use different strips.

Each fit starts with uniform weights and performs four reweighting iterations with $\sigma=18$~px. At iteration $t$, the residuals of the current line give the new weights
\begin{equation}
    e_i^{(t)}=x_i-m^{(t)}y_i-q^{(t)},\qquad
    w_i^{(t+1)}=\frac{1}{1+(e_i^{(t)}/\sigma)^2},
    \label{eq:c6_reweight}
\end{equation}
and the line is refitted with \texttt{numpy.polyfit} of degree one. This function multiplies the unsquared residuals by the weights~\cite{numpyPolyfitDocumentation}, so each iteration solves
\begin{equation}
    (m,q)=\mathop{\arg\min}_{m,q}
        \sum_i \left[w_i(x_i-my_i-q)\right]^2,
    \label{eq:c6_objective}
\end{equation}
and each squared residual is weighted by $w_i^2$. The weights are recomputed from the current line at every iteration, not accumulated, and the number of iterations is fixed.

Before the final fit, the accepted intervals are checked for a crossing of the two edges. The check changes the result only when the modeled width falls below $d_{\min}$ between the lowest and the highest accepted interval. Let $\Delta m=m_R-m_L$ and $\Delta q=q_R-q_L$. If $\Delta m>10^{-6}$, the edges converge upward and the modeled width reaches the minimum interval width $d_{\min}=8$~px at $y_\times=(d_{\min}-\Delta q)/\Delta m$. When $y_\times$ lies below the highest accepted interval, the intervals above $y_\times$ are discarded and both edges are fitted again. This check is repeated at most three times, and it ends earlier when no crossing is found, when fewer than three intervals would remain, or when an edge has fewer than two samples. The remaining intervals are used in all the following steps.

The final residual of each sample is its absolute horizontal distance from its fitted edge. With $\bar e$ the mean over the samples of both edges, the fit score is
\begin{equation}
    F=\operatorname{clip}(1-\bar e/\sigma,0,1).
    \label{eq:c6_fit_score}
\end{equation}
The reweighting reduces the effect of isolated outliers, but it cannot correct a wrong association that produces a consistent wrong line.

\section{Validation, confidence, and output}
\label{sec:c6_output}

The modeled corridor width is $D(y)=x_R(y)-x_L(y)=\Delta m\,y+\Delta q$, and rows with $D(y)\geq d_{\min}$ are valid. Let $y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{top}}$ and $y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{bot}}$ be the smallest and largest heights of the edge samples. If $|\Delta m|<10^{-6}$, the edges are parallel, the check requires $\Delta q\geq d_{\min}$, and the valid range goes from $y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{top}}$ to $H-1$. Otherwise, the width reaches $d_{\min}$ at $y_\times$ and the valid range is
\begin{equation}
    y_v^{\mathrm{top}}=
    \begin{cases}
        \max(y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{top}},y_\times) & \Delta m>0\\
        y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{top}} & \Delta m<0
    \end{cases},\qquad
    y_v^{\mathrm{bot}}=
    \begin{cases}
        H-1 & \Delta m>0\\
        \min(H-1,y_\times) & \Delta m<0
    \end{cases}.
\end{equation}
In this case the fit is rejected if the crossing lies inside the observed range, $y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{top}}+0.05H<y_\times<y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{bot}}$, which indicates swapped edges or an association across different regions. It is also rejected if the valid range is shorter than $0.10(H-y_0)$, which is 11.2~px on the robot. A crossing outside this interval can only shorten the valid range.

The regularity of the corridor width is measured on the accepted intervals that are not clipped on either side, because the width of a clipped interval is not the corridor width. With $z_j=r_j-\ell_j$ for these $K$ intervals in association order,
\begin{equation}
    E_D=\frac{Q_{0.90}\left(\{|z_{j+2}-2z_{j+1}+z_j|\}_{j=1}^{K-2}\right)}
                   {\max(\operatorname{median}_j|z_j|,1)},
    \qquad A=\operatorname{clip}(1-E_D/0.30,0,1),
    \label{eq:c6_width_score}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{0.90}$ is the 90th percentile. If $K<3$, $E_D=0$. A width that changes linearly from one interval to the next gives zero second differences. Because the differences are taken in sample order and not per pixel of height, skipped strips and excluded clipped intervals can also produce nonzero values.

The tracking score $T$ is the number of accepted intervals divided by the number of strips whose center lies at or below $y_v^{\mathrm{top}}$, limited to 1. $G$ depends on the longest run $g_{\max}$ of strips skipped between two accepted intervals. The confidence is
\begin{equation}
    G=\operatorname{clip}(1-g_{\max}/3,0,1),\qquad
    C_4=0.3125\,T+0.3125\,F+0.25\,A+0.125\,G.
    \label{eq:c6_confidence4}
\end{equation}
The result is \texttt{lost} if the geometric check fails, if $\bar e>2\sigma$, or if $C_4<0.38$. Otherwise it is \texttt{degraded} for $C_4<0.60$ and \texttt{ok} for $C_4\geq0.60$. $C_4$ is a heuristic score, not a calibrated probability.

For a \texttt{lost} result, the output mask is empty and no centerline model is returned. Otherwise, the centerline is drawn from the first row of the highest accepted strip, or from $y_v^{\mathrm{top}}$ if this is lower in the image, down to $y_v^{\mathrm{bot}}$. In each row its column is clipped to $[0,W-1]$, and the drawn line is dilated with a $3\times3$ elliptical kernel. The controller reads the lateral error from the analytic model $x_C(y)$, not from the drawn mask (Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}).

\section{Baseline extractors}
\label{sec:c6_baseline_algorithms}

Method~4 is compared with three baseline extractors. They have the same interface as Method~4, so switching extractor only changes the module imported by the controller.

\subsection{Center points}
\label{subsec:c6_center_baseline}

The center-point extractor (\texttt{pf\_centri.py}) uses the same mask cleaning and interval extraction as Method~4, but with $N=24$ strips and a ROI that starts at $y_0=\lfloor 0.30H\rfloor$. Each interval is reduced to its midpoint $c=(\ell+r)/2$ and its width $d=r-\ell+1$. The seed is the interval of the bottom six strips with the most path pixels, with ties broken by width. Intervals are associated upward with the cost
\begin{equation}
    J_{\text{center}} = |c^{(k+1)} - c^{(k)}| + 0.4\, |d^{(k+1)} - d^{(k)}|,
    \label{eq:c6_center_cost}
\end{equation}
under the jump limit $|c^{(k+1)} - c^{(k)}| \le \max(28, 0.5\, d^{(k)})$, with the same stopping rule and the same minimum of three intervals as Method~4. One line is fitted to the midpoints with four iterations of the weights of Eq.~\eqref{eq:c6_reweight}. Unlike Method~4, the initial weights are proportional to the pixel count of each interval (normalized to mean one), the weights multiply across iterations, and clipped intervals are not excluded. The score is $0.30T+0.35F+0.25A+0.10G$, with $T$ computed over all strips, $A$ on the widths $d$ of all accepted intervals, and $G$ on the longest run of skipped strips, including the run that ends the association. The result is \texttt{lost} if the mean residual exceeds $2\sigma$ or if the score is below 0.38, and \texttt{degraded} if it is below 0.60. The line is drawn from the top of the highest accepted strip to the bottom of the image. Because the midpoints are formed before the fit, a disturbance on one edge moves the midpoint and enters the centerline.

\subsection{Column histogram}
\label{subsec:c6_histogram_baseline}

The column-histogram extractor (\texttt{pf\_histogram.py}) is a simplified version of the column projection of SegMin. SegMin sums a plant mask, accumulated over recent frames and refined with depth, along the image columns, smooths the profile, and takes its minimum~\cite{navone2025gpsfree}. \texttt{pf\_histogram} uses the path mask of a single frame, without depth or smoothing, and takes the maximum. Its ROI starts at $y_0=\lfloor 0.30H\rfloor$, and the mask is cleaned with the same opening and closing as Method~4, keeping components of at least 300 pixels. The column counts of its cleaned ROI mask $\widetilde M_h$ are
\begin{equation}
    p(x) = \sum_{y=0}^{H-y_0-1} \widetilde M_h(y, x), \qquad x \in [0, W-1].
    \label{eq:c6_histogram}
\end{equation}
The target column $x_{\text{target}}$ is the center of the widest run of columns whose count equals the maximum $p_{\max}$. The confidence is $p_{\max}/(H-y_0)$, with the same thresholds as Method~4 (0.38 and 0.60). The output is the vertical line $x=x_{\text{target}}$ from $y_0$ to the bottom of the image, so the method gives a lateral position but no heading.

\subsection{RANSAC edges}
\label{subsec:c6_ransac_baseline}

RANSAC line fitting is used in agricultural row detection on points of the crop rows~\cite{bai2023vision,sensors2025deeplabv3}. The RANSAC extractor (\texttt{pf\_ransac.py}) applies it to the edges of the segmented corridor, without strips or bottom-up association. Its ROI starts at $y_0=\lfloor 0.30H\rfloor$ and the mask is cleaned as in Method~4. Among the components with at least 500 pixels, it keeps the one that maximizes its area plus ten times the top row of its bounding box, which favors large components that start lower in the image. In each ROI row of this component with width of at least 10~px, the leftmost and rightmost pixels are edge points, and at least ten rows are required.

Each edge is fitted with RANSAC~\cite{fischler1981ransac}: in 100 trials, two points drawn by a generator with fixed seed (42) define a line, and the points at a horizontal distance of at most 18~px from it are inliers. The line with the most inliers is refitted by least squares on its inliers, and the centerline is the mean of the two edge lines, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:c6_line}. The result is \texttt{lost} if the mean inlier residual exceeds 36~px, if the mean inlier ratio of the two edges is below 0.30, or if the score $0.35\,(\text{inlier ratio})+0.30\,(\text{fit score})+0.20\,(\text{vertical coverage})+0.15\,(\text{width score})$ is below 0.38, and \texttt{degraded} if this score is below 0.60. The fit score is $\operatorname{clip}(1-\bar e_{\mathrm{in}}/18,0,1)$, with $\bar e_{\mathrm{in}}$ the average of the mean inlier residuals of the two edges. The vertical coverage is the fraction of ROI rows with edge points. The width score is $\operatorname{clip}(1-E_w/0.30,0,1)$, with $E_w$ the median absolute difference between the widths of consecutive edge rows divided by their median width. The line is drawn from the highest edge point to the bottom of the image. Because the seed is fixed, repeated runs on the same frame give the same result.

\label{sec:c6_tradeoffs_and_limitations}

Method~4 has four structural limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Seed dependence.} If the bottom six strips contain no interval, for example because the mask in front of the robot is missing, the frame is rejected even if the upper part of the corridor is segmented. Within a chain the association is greedy, so at a fork the chain follows the branch with the lowest cost in the next strip, even if that branch ends soon after.
    \item \textbf{Thresholds in pixels.} The jump limit, the merging gap, the minimum widths and areas, and $\sigma$ are in pixels of the $224\times224$ mask, so they depend on the resolution and on the camera mounting. \textcolor{red}{Camera pitch and roll on uneven ground move the corridor in the image, and a displacement above the jump limit stops the association early.}
    \item \textbf{Straight edges.} Both edges are straight lines, so curved rows and headland turns are not represented. Li et al.~\cite{li2022multiroi} use a cubic B-spline for curved paths.
    \item \textbf{One-sided gaps.} Each accepted interval provides both edges. Where plants are missing on one side, the interval can widen or merge with a nearby region, and if the displacement exceeds the jump limit for more than three consecutive strips, the association stops. If the missing side extends the interval to the image border, the endpoint on that side is clipped, and the frame is rejected when an edge has fewer than two non-clipped samples. The association stop and the clipped-edge rejection do not exist in \texttt{pf\_histogram}, which tracks no edges.
\end{enumerate}
