\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{cap:conclusions}

\section{Main contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

This thesis studied vision-based local guidance between crop rows. It covered the choice of the segmentation network, the annotation of its training data with SAM~3, the extraction of the navigation reference from the mask, and the test of the complete chain on a robot. The main contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Architecture comparison for robotic segmentation:} FCN, DeepLabV3, and LR-ASPP were compared on manually annotated lettuce images. LR-ASPP was selected. Its mIoU is 1.08 percentage points lower, and on the training GPU it is 7 to 11 times faster (8.21~ms against 59.10~ms and 86.90~ms).

\item \textbf{Automatic dataset annotation with SAM~3:} Training labels were generated with SAM~3 text prompts and used without any manual correction. Networks trained on these labels for lettuce were compared with a network trained on manual masks, and the binary labeling was then extended to the complete lettuce, tomato, and cucumber collection to train the network deployed on the robot.

\item \textbf{Evaluation of output label configurations:} Networks with four-class, three-class, and binary path outputs, trained on SAM~3 lettuce labels, were compared against the manual ground truth. Their path IoU values differ by less than 0.5 percentage points in the realistic scenario. The binary configuration was adopted for the robot because the path is the only class used by the extractors.

\item \textbf{Edge-based geometric extractor and its evaluation:} Strip-IRLS converts the corridor mask into a navigation reference. It extracts intervals in horizontal strips, associates them bottom-up and selects the chain that gives the most edge samples, excludes the samples clipped by the image border, and fits the left and right edges separately with iteratively reweighted least squares. It was compared with three baseline extractors (Center points, Column histogram, RANSAC edges) on synthetic masks with exact ground truth and on real frames of three crops with manually annotated corridor edges.

\item \textbf{Edge regression network, a first experiment:} EdgeNet, a small network trained on synthetic masks, takes the binary mask and regresses the two corridor edges. It has the same interface as the geometric extractors and was compared with them in the same tests.

\item \textbf{Integration on the robot and field tests:} The segmentation network, the five extractors and the controller run in one ROS~2 node on the Jetson AGX Orin of the robot (Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}). The controller filters the error with an exponential moving average and a rejection of isolated jumps, and applies a deadband and a proportional steering law. Without the debug video, the callback takes 11.3 to 26.2~ms depending on the extractor, less than the 33.3~ms frame period of the camera. All five extractors were tested in closed loop in a tomato row, with the trajectory of the robot measured by a total station (Section~\ref{sec:field_validation}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results_conclusions}

On lettuce, the network trained on manual labels has a path IoU of 0.9532 on the complete test set and 0.9587 in the realistic scenario. The networks trained only on uncorrected SAM~3 labels reach 0.8966 to 0.9013 in the realistic scenario, but 0.39 to 0.41 on the complete test set. In the realistic scenario the four-class network has an mIoU of 0.5925. Fully automatic labels therefore give a path IoU of about 0.90 between parallel rows, 5.7 to 6.2 percentage points below manual labels. They do not replace manual annotation when all four classes are needed or for viewpoints the robot cannot reach. The multi-crop binary configuration used on the robot needs no manual annotation and reaches 0.9071 in the realistic scenario and 0.6369 on the complete test set, when the lettuce test images are excluded from its training data. The segmentation of the deployed network was checked visually on recorded videos of the rows. The binary labels need one text prompt and give the extractors the only class they use. The plant and covering-material classes are lost, and other tasks may need them.

No extractor is best on every test. On the 255 annotated frames Strip-IRLS fails on 11, RANSAC edges on 9, EdgeNet on 14, and Center points and Column histogram on 52 each. On the synthetic masks each extractor has its own failure case (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}).

In the field tests the robot followed a tomato row with sparse plants and poor light, from a field not present in the training data, with each of the five extractors. The measured lateral distance from the reference centerline stayed within 13.6~cm on the 8~m runs and within 20.4~cm on the full row. Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges and Center points differ from the mean of the other four extractors by 1.1 to 2.1~cm (root mean square), so these tests do not rank them. The lateral distance they have in common varies by about 24~cm along the full row, and it comes from the masks predicted in this field or from the reference. Column histogram differs by 6.8~cm on the short runs, because it classified as \texttt{ok} also the frames in which its centerline was at the image border.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The main limitations of the work are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Structural limits of Strip-IRLS:} The dependence on the seed in the bottom strips, the number of parameters and the effect of missing plants on one side are described in Section~\ref{sec:c5_tradeoffs_and_limitations}. On the synthetic masks the seed and the merging gap cause the gross errors in \texttt{hole\_bottom\_tall} and \texttt{adjacent+offset} (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}).

\item \textbf{Straight-line model:} Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges, Center points and EdgeNet model the corridor with straight lines. They cannot follow curved rows or the headland turn, where the crop boundaries end or diverge.

\item \textbf{Segmentation ground truth:} Segmentation accuracy was measured against manual ground truth only on lettuce. Tomato and cucumber have no manual ground truth. The deployed weights have no independent IoU, because their training data include the lettuce test images, so the multi-crop configuration was evaluated through a separate network trained without them. The random image-level split takes training and test images from the same lettuce folders, each recorded along one path. No test image is used for training, but the reported IoU values may be optimistic for paths not seen during training.

\item \textbf{SAM~3 labels:} Visual inspection showed that SAM~3 segmented the plastic mulch film poorly and assigned the greenhouse plastic cover to the covering class. Its path masks also cover adjacent paths and soil outside the corridor, especially in images taken from unusual viewpoints or where the path between the rows is not clearly visible, while the manual masks mark only the central corridor. The labels were used without correction, so these errors enter the training data. Their extent and their effect on the IoU were not measured.

\item \textbf{Annotation effort:} The time saved by automatic labeling with respect to manual annotation was not measured.

\item \textbf{Offline test data:} The synthetic masks are straight corridors whose edges have irregularities of a few pixels, while the real predictions often have rounded boundaries. Only 255 real frames have an annotated ground truth, marked once by one person. The frames where the edges could not be marked or gave no usable centerline, which are among the most difficult, are not in the set. Most annotated frames come from paths used to train the network, so their masks are probably cleaner than predictions on new paths.

\item \textbf{Parameters of the geometric extractors:} The parameters of Strip-IRLS and of the baselines were chosen for the camera, its mounting and the fields of this work (Section~\ref{sec:c5_parameter_setting}). With another camera, mounting or crop, they may need to be adjusted.

\item \textbf{Confidence of the extractors:} The confidence scores are heuristic and not calibrated. Most gross errors of Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet are classified as \texttt{ok}, so a wrong line that persists over consecutive frames reaches the command.

\item \textbf{Field tests:} The tests were made in one row of one crop on one day, with three short runs of 8~m and one run along the full row per extractor. The reference of the row center was placed by eye at five points, so the measured lateral distance also contains its error.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future_work}

\subsection{Dataset and annotation}
\label{subsec:future_annotation}

\begin{itemize}
\item Measure the annotation time of manual and automatic workflows, and test whether a light correction of the SAM~3 labels improves the trained networks.
\item Split the data by folder rather than by image, so that the test paths are not represented in training, and repeat the evaluation of all networks.
\item Prepare manual ground-truth masks for tomato and cucumber to evaluate the deployed network on those crops directly.
\item Add images of fields like the one of the field tests, with sparse plants and poor light, to the training data.
\item Select the images to annotate manually with active learning.
\item Compare SAM~3 with other foundation models, or with SAM variants fine-tuned on agricultural images, for the automatic labels.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Segmentation and model deployment}
\label{subsec:future_segmentation}

\begin{itemize}
\item Compare other lightweight segmentation architectures with LR-ASPP.
\item Reduce the inference time on the Jetson AGX Orin with quantization and model compression.
\item Use previous frames to make the segmentation consistent in time.
\item Estimate the uncertainty of the segmentation, to detect the frames where it is not reliable.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Geometric extraction}
\label{subsec:future_geometric}

\begin{itemize}
\item Extend the line model to piecewise linear or polynomial edges, to follow curved rows and headland turns.
\item Search the corridor from the top of the ROI when the bottom-up seeding fails, and handle a corridor that rises from a full-width band of path.
\item Annotate more real frames, from new recordings and by more than one annotator, so that the differences of a few frames between the best extractors can be resolved and the annotation error can be measured.
\item Calibrate the confidence of each extractor, EdgeNet included, on annotated real frames, so that a wrong line classified as \texttt{ok} is detected before it reaches the controller.
\item Extend the generator of EdgeNet to other cameras, mountings and crops, so that a new scenario needs a new training set and not a new tuning of geometric thresholds, and evaluate EdgeNet on annotated frames of new recordings.
\item Adapt the thresholds of the extractors to the pitch and roll of the robot, measured by an IMU.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{System integration and field validation}
\label{subsec:future_integration}

\begin{itemize}
\item Repeat the field tests in more rows, crops and lighting conditions, with longer runs. Measure the reference at more points along the row, so that its error can be separated from that of the robot.
\item Track the line parameters over time, for example with a Kalman filter driven by the motion of the robot, so that guidance continues during reference losses longer than the 0.5~s hold of the controller.
\item Fuse the camera with other sensors, such as depth and IMU, to keep a valid reference when the camera view is degraded.
\item Detect obstacles on the path. Near the end of the short runs Strip-IRLS classified 232 of 241 frames as \texttt{ok} while the total station and the operator stood on the path (Section~\ref{subsec:navigation_results}).
\item Add global path planning.
\end{itemize}
