\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{cap:conclusions}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This thesis built a camera-based guidance chain for a robot between crop rows: a segmentation network trained on labels generated by SAM~3, an extractor that turns the mask into a reference line, and a proportional controller, all running on the Jetson AGX Orin of the robot.

\textbf{Automatic labels.} Networks trained only on uncorrected SAM~3 labels reach a path IoU of about 0.90 between parallel rows, about 6 percentage points below a network trained on manual labels. SAM~3 can label some classes or scenes poorly, such as the covering material, and it also labels as path the ground outside the corridor, so whether automatic labels are suitable depends on the task. For the path between parallel rows, the class used here, they were enough: the robot stayed in the row in all the field runs.

\textbf{Architecture.} LR-ASPP was chosen over FCN and DeepLabV3. Its mIoU is 1.08 percentage points lower, and it is 7 to 11 times faster.

\textbf{Label configuration.} The path IoU of the four-class, three-class and binary networks differs by less than 0.5 percentage points. The binary configuration was adopted because the extractors use only the path.

\textbf{Extractors.} On masks with one corridor and on the annotated real frames, Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet and RANSAC edges have few gross errors, and these tests do not rank them. With adjacent corridors RANSAC edges has many gross errors, while Strip-IRLS and EdgeNet have few. Center points loses many more frames, and Column histogram has the most gross errors. EdgeNet, trained only on synthetic masks, performs like the geometric extractors and is the fastest of the three. It has no geometric parameters to set by hand, so it can replace the hand tuning of geometric thresholds.

\textbf{Field tests.} The five extractors were tested on the robot with the same complete chain, from the camera to the steering commands, and only the extractor changed between runs. All five followed a tomato row of a field not present in the training data of the network, a difficult case with sparse plants and poor light, and the measured distance from the reference centerline stayed within 13.6~cm on the short runs and 20.4~cm on the full row. Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges and Center points drove the robot along almost the same path, and the best extractors fail mostly on the same badly segmented frames. The quality of the segmentation therefore matters more than the choice among these extractors.

\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:limitations_future}

\paragraph{Segmentation.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ground truth:} the segmentation was measured against manual masks only on lettuce, with a random split by image, so training and test images are different frames of the same paths and the results can be optimistic. Manual masks of tomato and cucumber and a split by path would give an independent measure of the deployed network.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Extractors.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Baselines:} they are implementations of this work and differ from the original methods. \textit{Column histogram}, for example, is a simplified version of SegMin (Section~\ref{sec:c5_baseline_algorithms}). Their results may therefore not hold for the published methods. A comparison with the original implementations, where available, would show the difference.
\item \textbf{Seed:} Strip-IRLS grows the corridor from its bottom strips (Section~\ref{sec:c5_tradeoffs_and_limitations}), so a corridor whose bottom is empty, split or part of a full-width band of path can make it fail. A global method that uses the whole mask, such as \textit{EdgeNet}, does not need a seed and could handle these cases.
\item \textbf{Offline test data:} the synthetic masks are straight corridors whose edges have irregularities of a few pixels, while the real predictions often have rounded boundaries. Most of the 255 annotated frames come from paths used to train the network, so their masks are probably cleaner than on new paths, and these frames, which are not independent, are too few to rank the best extractors. More frames from new recordings, marked by more than one annotator, would resolve their differences and measure the annotation error.
\item \textbf{Confidence:} the confidence scores are heuristic and do not always follow the error. EdgeNet returns \texttt{lost} on 25 synthetic masks with adjacent corridors where its line is correct (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}), while all the gross errors of Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges on the annotated frames are classified as \texttt{ok} (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_annotated}). A confidence calibrated against the error on annotated real frames could keep the correct lines and return \texttt{lost} on the wrong ones.
\item \textbf{Parameters:} the thresholds of the geometric extractors were tuned by hand for the camera and mounting of this robot, while EdgeNet learns from its generator. Tests with a different camera or mounting would show whether the results of the geometric extractors degrade more than those of EdgeNet. These thresholds are fixed, and they could be made to change while the robot moves, for example with its pitch and roll measured by an IMU.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Field tests.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Extent:} the tests were made in one row of one crop on one day, and the reference centerline was placed by eye at five points. Tests in more rows, crops and light conditions, with a reference measured at more points, would separate better the error of the reference from that of the robot.
\item \textbf{Lost or wrong reference:} when the reference is lost for more than 0.5~s the robot stops, and a wrong segmentation that persists over consecutive frames can steer it towards the plants. Handling both cases better is left to future work.
\item \textbf{Obstacles:} the chain does not detect obstacles on the path. They could be detected in the depth image of the RealSense D435, which this work does not use.
\end{itemize}
