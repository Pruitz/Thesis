\chapter{Experimental Validation}
\label{cap:experiments}

This chapter reports the experimental evaluation of the segmentation models, geometric extractors, and integrated navigation system. It covers offline evaluation using recorded images and, if completed, closed-loop field trials.

\section{Offline evaluation}
\label{sec:offline_evaluation}

\subsection{Segmentation metrics}
\label{subsec:segmentation_metrics}

Segmentation performance is measured using intersection over union (IoU) for each semantic class. For a class $c$, IoU is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{IoU}_c=
    \frac{\operatorname{TP}_c}
         {\operatorname{TP}_c+\operatorname{FP}_c+\operatorname{FN}_c},
    \label{eq:iou}
\end{equation}
where true positives, false positives, and false negatives are computed against the manual reference masks.

Mean IoU (mIoU) averages the per-class IoU values. For the four-class configuration, mIoU includes background, plants, path, and covering material. Path IoU is the task-specific metric most relevant for navigation.

\subsubsection{Architecture comparison results}
\label{subsubsec:architecture_results}

The architecture comparison in Section~\ref{subsec:c5_architecture_results} reports, on the 77 test images of the manual lettuce dataset, a path IoU of 0.9532 for LR-ASPP, against 0.9654 for FCN and 0.9686 for DeepLabV3. The recorded GPU inference times are 8.21~ms, 59.10~ms, and 86.90~ms. LR-ASPP accepts an mIoU about 1.1 percentage points lower in exchange for 7.2 to 10.6 times shorter recorded times.

\label{subsec:geometric_accuracy}

The synthetic masks have $224\times224$ pixels. Each one is a straight corridor in perspective, defined by its bottom center, vanishing point, bottom half-width and apex row, with irregular edges (a slow undulation of a few pixels and a fine grain of 1--2~px). The first set has 21 base scenarios with one disturbance each, two of them without a corridor (an empty mask and a mask that is all path), and 4 scenarios with adjacent corridors or a curved corridor, layouts that were not present in the test field. The second set takes its shapes from 100 masks predicted on frames evenly spaced in the recording \texttt{pomodori2} (9465 compressed images of the topic \texttt{/camera/camera/color/\allowbreak image\_raw/\allowbreak compressed}, taken in a greenhouse with tomato plants), segmented as in the controller node (Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}): apex low in the image, wide corridor, large-scale wavy edges, bottom corners cut by the nearby plants, and a band of path across the bottom of the image with no corridor. Table~\ref{tab:c8_scenarios} lists the scenarios. The masks generated with seed 0 were used during the development of the extractors, and the results reported here are computed on a separate set generated with seed 2027.

The real masks were predicted by the network deployed on the robot on the images of 13 sequences of the SAM~3 dataset (Chapter~\ref{cap:segmentation}): 866 of tomato in 7 sequences, 268 of cucumber in 2 sequences and 470 of lettuce in 4 sequences. They are reduced from $640\times480$ to $224\times224$ pixels with nearest-neighbor interpolation. These images belong to the training data of the network, so the masks are probably cleaner than those of images not seen in training. 348 masks without path pixels and four lettuce sequences in which the network predicted almost no path (7 further masks) were removed. The annotated frames are described in Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_annotated}.

The error is $|\hat x-x_{\mathrm{true}}|$ at the row used by the controller, $y_{\mathrm{ref}}=156$ (Chapter~\ref{cap:platform}), and is computed only on the frames not classified as \texttt{lost}. In the tomato-shaped set the apex can lie below $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and the ground truth is the linear extension of the centerline, as the extractors extend their lines. A gross error is an error above 15~px (13\% of the half-width of the image). For the masks without a corridor the expected status is \texttt{lost}. A failure is a frame that is \texttt{lost} or has a gross error. All five extractors are deterministic: RANSAC edges draws its samples from a generator with a fixed seed, and the others do not sample. Their times on the Jetson AGX Orin are in Section~\ref{subsec:timing}.

\subsubsection{Results on the annotated real frames}
\label{subsubsec:geometric_annotated}

The annotated frames give a ground truth on real data. From each of the 14 real sequences, 22 frames evenly spaced in the sequence were selected (308 frames). In a browser tool written for this purpose, the author marked two points on each corridor edge in the $640\times480$ photo, with the predicted mask shown as a semi-transparent overlay, along the line where the plants meet the ground. Each edge is the line through its two points, scaled to the $224\times224$ mask as in the controller node, and the true centerline is the mean of the two edge lines, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:c6_line}. The error is measured at $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$ as for the synthetic masks.

The sequences were split before the evaluation into a development part (\texttt{cetrioli\_grandi}, \texttt{insalata\_grande1}, \texttt{insalata\_media1}, \texttt{pomodori\_maturi1}, \texttt{pomodori\_maturi3}, \texttt{pomodori\_maturi5} and \texttt{pomodori2}) and a test part (the other seven), each with frames of the three crops. \texttt{pomodori2} is in the development part because the thresholds of RANSAC edges were chosen on its frames. The development frames were used to choose the version of Strip-IRLS. The test frames were evaluated once for the other extractors, after they had been fixed, and four times for Strip-IRLS: with $\rho=0.50$ and gap 30~px (4.1\%, 5 of 122 gross errors), with $\rho=0.30$ and gap 30~px (2.5\%, 3 of 122), with $\rho=0.30$, gap 40~px and the strips of 9 rows of an earlier version (3.3\%, 4 of 122), and with the final version, reported below. The final version tiles the ROI exactly with strips of 10 rows (Section~\ref{sec:c6_candidates}) and uses ten reweighting iterations. Both changes were checked on the development data, where the gross errors on the annotated frames stayed at 3 frames, and the test frames were evaluated after them. After this evaluation the merging gap was changed from 40 to 30~px, the value used on the robot, and no result on the annotated frames changed. The parameters were chosen on the development data. The annotator skipped 12 development frames and 22 test frames, mostly of tomato, where the edges could not be read. The tomato sequences of the SAM~3 dataset were recorded with a handheld camera, and in many of their frames the image is shaken or the segmentation is poor. In 10 development frames and 9 test frames one annotated edge is inclined by more than $60^\circ$ from the vertical. There the mean of the two lines in the form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:c6_edges} is not a usable centerline, and its value at $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$ can lie hundreds of pixels outside the image, so these frames are excluded from the error statistics. The threshold of $60^\circ$ was chosen on the development frames. 132 development frames and 123 test frames remain. None of them was annotated as a frame without a corridor.

Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges have the fewest failures on both parts. Their difference is a few frames and their order changes between the two parts, so these frames do not separate them. Center points and Column histogram fail on 24 to 28 frames per part. The network has few failures on the development part and 10 on the test part. The three gross errors of Strip-IRLS on the test frames are all in the tomato sequence \texttt{pomodori\_maturi6}, as is the one of RANSAC edges. Most gross errors of Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet are classified as \texttt{ok}, so their status does not warn the controller.

These results have four limits. The 22 frames of a sequence are not independent. On the test frames the gross errors of Strip-IRLS and RANSAC edges are all in one tomato sequence, and Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet have no gross errors on lettuce, so the comparison rests on about 100 frames. The annotation was made once by one person, with the predicted mask shown in overlay, which can draw the annotation toward the mask, and its error was not measured. The annotated centerline is therefore not exact like the synthetic one, and differences of a few pixels in the median error, which are 1.3 to 2.3~px for Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet, are not interpreted: the comparison relies on the gross errors. The ground truth is marked on the photo, so the measured error also contains the segmentation error, which is the same for all extractors. The excluded frames, those skipped and those with a nearly horizontal edge, are among the most difficult, so the reported rates are lower than on all frames.

Each extractor fails in different scenarios:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strip-IRLS.} In \texttt{hole\_bottom\_tall} a central hole 33 to 50~px wide splits the bottom of the corridor. Strip-IRLS merges columns separated by at most 30~px, so it does not join the two parts where the hole is wider. A hole wider than the gap and taller than the seed window (six strips of 10 rows) puts every seed in one of the two parts. The chain that starts from it stops where the parts join, because there the interval widens beyond the jump limit, and its edges are the two edges of that part. This causes gross errors in 2.0\% of the masks of this scenario. In \texttt{adjacent+offset} the merging gap joins the narrow upper part of the row between two corridors with the corridors beside it, and 4.2\% of the masks have a gross error.
    \item \textbf{EdgeNet.} Its errors are in the tomato-shaped scenarios where the apex lies below $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and the true centerline is near the image border (\texttt{pom\_ruotato}, \texttt{pom\_spostato}, \texttt{pom\_combinato}). There the value at $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is an extension of the two lines, and a small error on one edge becomes a large error. With adjacent corridors it has no gross errors, but it classifies only 31.5\% of these masks as \texttt{ok}, because the mask also contains the adjacent corridors and the intersection over union in its confidence is low. A first model, trained with an earlier generator without adjacent corridors and with the apex only between rows 30 and 100, had 21.3\% gross errors on the tomato-shaped set and 95.7\% in \texttt{adjacent+offset}, so the accuracy of the network depends on the coverage of its generator.
    \item \textbf{RANSAC edges.} It takes the first and last path pixel of each row of the selected component, so when an adjacent corridor belongs to that component its edge replaces the edge of the central corridor in those rows.
    \item \textbf{Center points.} It forms the midpoints of the intervals before the fit, so a disturbance on one side or in the middle of the corridor moves the midpoints and enters the centerline (\texttt{bulge\_one\_side}, \texttt{hole\_bottom\_tall}, \texttt{pom\_spostato}). It returns a line on the mask that is all path. With adjacent corridors it follows one interval per strip and has almost no gross errors.
    \item \textbf{Column histogram.} It returns a vertical line, so its error grows with the offset and the rotation of the robot and on the curved corridor. Its confidence is the maximum column count divided by the ROI height, which is low when the path starts below the middle of the image, so it rejects 30.6\% of the tomato-shaped masks and all those of \texttt{pom\_piccolo}.
\end{itemize}
On the curved corridor, the straight-line extractors keep the error below 15~px at $y_{\mathrm{ref}}$, but their lines do not follow the curve. As on the annotated frames, most gross errors of Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet are classified as \texttt{ok}.

\subsubsection{Choice of the parameters of Strip-IRLS}
\label{subsubsec:c8_parameters}

The ROI start and the merging gap of Strip-IRLS were chosen on the development data, the seed 0 masks and the development part of the annotated frames. Moving the requested ROI start from $\rho=0.50$ to $\rho=0.30$ at a gap of 30~px lowered the gross errors on the base scenarios from 6.0\% to 0.5\% and on \texttt{hole\_bottom\_tall} from 100\% to 1.0\%. On the development part of the annotated frames it moved them from 1.6\% (2 of 126) to 2.3\% (3 of 128). Table~\ref{tab:c8_gap} shows the effect of the merging gap with $\rho=0.30$: a larger gap joins more holes inside the corridor but also more rows between adjacent corridors, and on the annotated frames it makes no difference from 20 to 40~px. A gap of 40~px was chosen with the strip geometry of the earlier version. With the final geometry the table shows that 30~px has fewer errors on \texttt{adjacent+offset} (3.1\% against 15.0\%) and 1.0\% instead of 0.0\% on \texttt{hole\_bottom\_tall}, so the final version uses 30~px, the value used on the robot. Variants with an MSAC fit of the edges~\cite{torr2000mlesac} and with parameters chosen on the synthetic errors had fewer synthetic errors, but they were not better on the development part of the annotated frames, and they were not kept.

\section{Field validation}
\label{sec:field_validation}

Field validation evaluates the integrated navigation system on the physical robot Demetra operating autonomously inside crop rows. Unlike offline evaluation, which isolates the geometric extractor on recorded images, closed-loop field tests exercise the full chain from camera acquisition through segmentation, geometric fitting, error conditioning, and steering actuation in the presence of real-world disturbances.

\subsection{Ground-truth reference: laser sensor}
\label{subsec:ground_truth_laser}

To assess the robot's physical centering independently of the onboard vision pipeline under test, closed-loop field validation integrates an auxiliary laser sensor. 

\todobox{Dettagli del sensore laser utilizzato per i test in campo}{%
Inserire le specifiche esatte del sensore laser impiegato nei test:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Modello, costruttore e tipologia di misura (ad es. sensore di distanza laser, barriera ottica o profilometro).
  \item Configurazione di montaggio sul robot Demetra (posizione e orientamento).
  \item Interfaccia di comunicazione e frequenza di campionamento.
\end{itemize}
}

The laser measurement provides an independent reference signal to monitor the robot's lateral alignment along the crop row and evaluate whether the trajectory remains within the allowable tolerance corridor.

\subsection{Field evaluation metrics}
\label{subsec:field_metrics}

The following metrics are computed for each test run. All algorithms under comparison (Strip-IRLS, EdgeNet, RANSAC edges, Center points and Column histogram) are evaluated on the same physical row under equivalent conditions.

\subsubsection{Laser centering rate}
\label{subsubsec:laser_centering}

The laser centering rate $P_{\mathrm{laser}}$ quantifies the fraction of the autonomous run during which the robot remains centered within the laser reference corridor:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{laser}} = \frac{T_{\mathrm{on}}}{T_{\mathrm{total}}} \times 100 \; [\%],
    \label{eq:laser_centering}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{on}}$ is the cumulative time with positive laser detection and $T_{\mathrm{total}}$ is the total duration of the autonomous run. A higher $P_{\mathrm{laser}}$ indicates that the guidance algorithm maintains the robot closer to the physical corridor center for a larger fraction of the mission.

\subsubsection{Number of centering losses}
\label{subsubsec:centering_losses}

The number of centering loss events $N_{\mathrm{exit}}$ counts how many times the laser detector transitions from active ($1$) to inactive ($0$) during a run. Each event corresponds to the robot drifting outside the acceptable centering band. Frequent transitions indicate oscillatory tracking behavior, even if the overall centering rate $P_{\mathrm{laser}}$ remains acceptable.

\subsubsection{Mission completion rate}
\label{subsubsec:mission_completion}

The mission completion rate $S_{\mathrm{mission}}$ measures the fraction of the test row traversed autonomously without requiring manual intervention or emergency stop:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{mission}} = \frac{L_{\mathrm{auto}}}{L_{\mathrm{row}}} \times 100 \; [\%],
    \label{eq:mission_completion}
\end{equation}
where $L_{\mathrm{row}}$ is the total length of the test row and $L_{\mathrm{auto}}$ is the distance covered before any intervention. A run terminated by collision with crop plants or by operator emergency stop records $S_{\mathrm{mission}} < 100\%$.

\subsubsection{Steering stability}
\label{subsubsec:steering_stability}

Steering stability is evaluated from the angular velocity commands $\omega_k$ recorded on the \texttt{/cmd\_vel} topic. Two indicators are computed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The standard deviation of angular velocity, $\sigma_\omega = \mathrm{std}(\omega_k)$, which quantifies overall steering activity.
    \item The mean absolute jitter, $J_\omega = \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} |\omega_{k+1} - \omega_k|$, which isolates high-frequency direction reversals independent of steady-state turning.
\end{itemize}
Lower values of both indicators correspond to smoother robot motion and reduced mechanical wear on the CubeMars AK70-10 steering actuators.

\subsubsection{Effective forward speed}
\label{subsubsec:effective_speed}

The effective forward speed $v_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the ratio between the distance traversed and the elapsed time. If the error conditioning filter enters the temporal hold state and issues stop commands ($v = 0$), the effective speed drops below the nominal setpoint $v_{\mathrm{nom}} = 0.20\text{ m/s}$. This metric captures how frequently each algorithm loses the guidance reference and forces the robot to halt.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Segmentation performance}
\label{subsec:segmentation_discussion}

The architecture comparison supports LR-ASPP as an accuracy--speed compromise: on the manual lettuce test images it loses about 1.1 percentage points of mIoU with respect to FCN and DeepLabV3, with much shorter recorded inference times. IoU alone does not establish that a segmentation is sufficient for navigation. This is assessed through the geometric extraction and the robot tests.

The supervision comparison tested labels generated by SAM~3 and used without any manual correction. On lettuce, networks trained on these labels remain below the manual model (path IoU 0.9013 against 0.9587 for the binary network in the realistic scenario) and lose most of their accuracy on the complete test set. Part of this gap may come from a difference in label definition: the manual masks mark only the central traversable corridor as path, whereas networks trained on SAM~3 labels can also segment adjacent or non-traversable corridors, which the IoU counts as false positives. This behavior was observed visually for the deployed network but was not quantified. The deployed network required no manual annotation of its training data, but the time saved with respect to manual labeling was not measured.

\subsection{Geometric extraction trade-offs}
\label{subsec:geometric_discussion}

No extractor is best on all the test sets. The results give the following picture of each geometric extractor:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strip-IRLS} has few failures on the annotated real frames. On the synthetic masks it fails when a hole wider than the merging gap and taller than the seed window splits the bottom of the corridor, and when the merging gap joins a narrow row between adjacent corridors. When the corridor rises from a band of path as wide as the image, it returns \texttt{lost} more often than RANSAC edges.
    \item \textbf{RANSAC edges} has few failures on the annotated real frames and rejects the band without a corridor most often. It fails with adjacent corridors, because the row extremes of the selected component can belong to the adjacent corridor.
    \item \textbf{Center points} follows one interval per strip and is accurate with adjacent corridors, but the midpoints formed before the fit carry the disturbances on one side into the centerline, and it fails more often on the annotated frames.
    \item \textbf{Column histogram} is the fastest extractor on the Jetson, but it returns a vertical line without heading, its error grows with the offset and the rotation of the robot, and it has the most failures on the annotated frames.
\end{itemize}

The variants of Strip-IRLS tuned on the synthetic development masks had fewer synthetic errors, but they were not better on the development part of the annotated real frames. Synthetic test sets written by the same author as the extractors find failure cases, but they can favor changes that do not carry over to real masks, so the choice between versions was made on real annotated frames.

For Strip-IRLS, RANSAC edges and EdgeNet, most gross errors, synthetic and real, are classified as \texttt{ok}. A calibrated confidence or a temporal consistency check between frames would give the controller a further protection besides the \texttt{lost} status.

\subsection{EdgeNet: first results}
\label{subsec:c8_network_discussion}

EdgeNet is a first experiment, and the results above are those of the second trained model (Section~\ref{sec:c6_network}). It has no geometric parameters in the extraction. On the development part of the annotated frames it has 4 failures, against 7 for Strip-IRLS and 6 for RANSAC edges, and on the test part 10, against 4 and 3 (Table~\ref{tab:c8_annotated}). On the synthetic masks with adjacent corridors and the curve it has no gross errors, where Strip-IRLS has 2.3\% and RANSAC edges 18.5\%, and its gross errors are in the tomato-shaped masks with the apex below the control row and the centerline near the image border. On the real masks it classifies 89.4\% of the frames as \texttt{ok}, between Strip-IRLS (87.7\%) and RANSAC edges (92.8\%). Its accuracy depends on how well its generator covers the masks met in the field (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_comparison}).

Adapting the network to a new crop or camera mounting means changing the generator and retraining it, not tuning thresholds in pixels. The design choices are not removed but moved into the generator, which has 16 disturbance probabilities and many hand-chosen ranges, and the status still depends on the thresholds 0.5, 8~px, 0.38 and 0.60. The loss gives double weight to row 156, the row of the evaluation. The geometric extractors also use the same parameters for all three crops, so one model for all crops is not an advantage of the network alone. The network was not tested with another camera or mounting. The second generator was written after the errors of the first model on the real masks had been inspected, and these masks include the sequences of the annotated test frames, although not their annotations.

At this early stage, with one model for the three crops, the network is close to the geometric extractors on the annotated real frames but not better. These first results make learning the extraction from the mask a promising direction. The failures above indicate what further work has to address: the extension of the lines when the apex lies below the control row, a confidence calibrated on real frames instead of the thresholds of Strip-IRLS, and a test on frames recorded after the generator was written.
