Autonomous navigation of agricultural robots between crop rows is difficult with satellite positioning alone: dense canopies and greenhouse structures degrade the GNSS signal, and RTK corrections and 3D LiDAR sensors are expensive. A monocular RGB camera is a low-cost alternative, provided that the drivable corridor can be segmented in each image and converted into a navigation reference.

This thesis develops a vision-based row-following pipeline for horticultural crops. A semantic segmentation network predicts the path between the rows, and a geometric extractor converts the predicted mask into an image-space reference line for the controller of the Demetra four-wheel-steering rover, which runs the network on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin through ONNX Runtime.

FCN, DeepLabV3, and LR-ASPP were first compared on a manually annotated dataset of 762 lettuce images. On the test images, LR-ASPP reaches an mIoU of 0.9145, against 0.9253 for both FCN and DeepLabV3. It has 3.2 million parameters, against 35.3 and 42.0 million, and a recorded offline GPU inference time of 8.21~ms, against 59.10~ms and 86.90~ms. It was therefore selected.

The thesis then investigates whether manual annotation can be replaced by labels generated automatically by SAM~3 from text prompts, without any manual correction. LR-ASPP networks trained on four-class, three-class, and binary SAM~3 labels of the same lettuce images were evaluated against the manual masks. On the test images taken between parallel rows, as seen by the robot, their path IoU is between 0.8966 and 0.9013, against 0.9587 for the network trained on manual masks. On the complete test set, which also contains images where the path between the greenhouse rows is not clearly visible, it drops to between 0.39 and 0.41. Visual inspection showed that the SAM~3 labels were less accurate in these images. Between parallel rows, the four-class network reaches an mIoU of 0.5925, against 0.9113 for the manual network, because the mulch film prompt segmented the film poorly and also labeled the greenhouse cover, which is background in the manual masks. The three-class network reaches a mean IoU of 0.8873 over the plant and path classes, against 0.9536 for the manual network. A binary network was then trained on the complete multi-crop SAM~3 dataset, with images of lettuce, tomato, and cucumber crops, excluding the lettuce test images. It reaches a path IoU of 0.9071 between parallel rows. The network deployed on the robot uses this configuration. Uncorrected automatic labels therefore provide usable supervision for the path class in the operating conditions, but they do not replace manual annotation when all classes are needed.

For the geometric stage, the thesis proposes an extractor that divides the region of interest into horizontal strips, associates the corridor intervals from the bottom of the image upwards, and fits the left and right edges separately with iteratively reweighted least squares. The centerline is the mean of the two edge lines. The extractor is compared with three baselines: a column histogram, a line fitted to the strip centers, and a RANSAC fit of the corridor contours. Fitting the two edges separately keeps a disturbance on one side out of the fit of the other side, but the method depends on finding the corridor near the vehicle, uses gates defined in image pixels, and assumes straight rows. The four extractors were tested in simulation and on the robot.
