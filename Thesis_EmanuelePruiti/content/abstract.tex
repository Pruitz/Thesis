Precision agriculture aims to increase crop yield and the efficiency of field operations while reducing waste. At the same time, the growing world population increases the demand for agricultural production, while the shortage of skilled labor and the rising cost of farm management push agriculture towards autonomous robots. Row-based crops are among the most studied applications of these robots.

Navigating between crop rows requires precise positioning, because the robot must stay within the corridor without damaging the plants. Satellite positioning alone does not guarantee this precision, since dense canopies and greenhouse structures degrade the GNSS signal and its accuracy. In addition, RTK corrections and 3D LiDAR sensors are expensive, which limits the large-scale adoption of agricultural robots. Camera-based methods are cheaper, but classical pipelines based on color thresholds are sensitive to illumination changes and weeds. Segmentation networks handle these conditions better, but they need large datasets annotated manually at pixel level, which take a long time to produce, and they must run fast on onboard hardware.

In this thesis, we propose a low-cost solution based on a monocular RGB camera and composed of two stages: segmentation and path extraction. In the first stage, a semantic segmentation network predicts the path between the rows. In the second stage, a path extractor estimates from the predicted mask the centerline of this path, which the robot follows to move along the row. The extractor divides the region of interest into horizontal strips, associates the corridor intervals from the bottom of the image upwards, and fits the left and right edges separately with iteratively reweighted least squares, so that short gaps and local errors in the predicted mask have a limited effect on the result. The centerline is the mean of the two edge lines.

The FCN, DeepLabV3, and LR-ASPP deep learning architectures were compared on a manually annotated dataset of lettuce images. LR-ASPP was selected for its speed: its accuracy is about 1\% lower than that of the other two networks, but its inference is 7 to 11 times faster, providing a lightweight execution on the onboard computer.

To reduce the annotation effort, we investigate whether manual annotation can be replaced by labels generated automatically by SAM~3 from text prompts, without manual correction. In operating conditions, on the images taken between parallel rows, networks trained on SAM~3 labels reach a path Intersection over Union about 6\% lower than the network trained on manual labels. Since the path is the only class used for navigation, the robot uses a binary network trained on the SAM~3 labels of lettuce, tomato, and cucumber crops, so the tomato and cucumber images required no manual annotation.

\textcolor{red}{The images were collected in two experimental campaigns, and a third campaign was dedicated to the tests on the robot. The proposed navigation method was compared with three state-of-the-art solutions. The tests showed that the robot can autonomously navigate in horticultural rows with a monocular RGB camera and a segmentation neural network trained using only automatic labels without manual correction. These results represent a possible robust solution for a precise motion inside the crops rows and a lower risk of damaging the crops, paving the way for further horticultural operations.}
